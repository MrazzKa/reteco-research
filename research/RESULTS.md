# Week 4 (GPU): dense retrieval over every corpus, hybrid, depth, error analysis

Machine: RTX 4060 Laptop (8 GB), WSL2, Python 3.14, torch 2.14.1+cu130, sentence-transformers 6.1.0, bm25s 0.3.13, faiss-cpu 1.15.1, ngt 2.8.0. Dense = `BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5` (rev 5c38ec7c), 512-token window, fp16 vectors, exact float32 search over the **whole** domain corpus. BM25 = bm25s-lucene as before. Both at depth 1000, ties broken by doc id. Every fusion weight and depth below was chosen on **train** and frozen before dev was scored. Macro = mean over domains.

Pool = BM25 top-kb ∪ dense top-kd. A document missing from one system's top-1000 gets that system's 1000th score (an upper bound) and no RRF contribution from it. Normalisation statistics come from each system's own top-1000 list. The best min-max and z-score settings are within 0.003 of each other on every sub-track, so the choice of normalisation hardly matters; the weight does (alpha 0.6–0.7, dense-leaning, in every case), and linear fusion beats the best RRF by 0.007–0.019 on train. Reranking the union pool with the same dense model is identical to dense retrieval (no new information) and is omitted.

## 6. E2 — explicit-date reranking (train): negative

Fixed rules over the BM25 top-100, no tuning: promote documents whose stated years fall inside the query's year span ±1 — across the whole list (`hard`), inside the top 20 or top 10, or fused with the BM25 order by RRF.

| variant | macro nDCG@10 | Δ vs BM25 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| BM25 (no reranking) | 0.1005 | — |
| hard | 0.0909 | -0.0096 |
| top20 | 0.0945 | -0.0060 |
| top10 | 0.0971 | -0.0034 |
| rrf | 0.0946 | -0.0059 |

Every variant loses, in every domain. Last week's feasibility check compared gold passages with *random* corpus documents (41% vs 11% year overlap); among BM25's topically similar top-100 the year signal no longer separates gold from non-gold. A date-aware system would have to use the signal inside the ranking function or in a recall stage, not as a post-hoc reorder.
