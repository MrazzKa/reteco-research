**Conversation so far:** Q: In coconuts, how do the three parts of the flower lead to the three holes we see on the shell? A: The three holes you see on a coconut shell actually come from its structure as a fruit. The hard shell, called the endocarp, naturally has three small spots or 'germination pores' at one end. Two of these are just plugged up, but one is functional—it's where the new coconut sprout would eventually grow out if the seed started to germinate. So those three holes are a direct result of how the coconut is designed to grow and reproduce.
